# Stats, charts and the insight pages

Covers every number the app shows about itself: the home dashboard tiles and mini charts, the stats screen,
day selection, the one events-based screen-time source, and the Willpower / Interventions insight pages.
**Read before touching `ui/screens/stats/`, `HomeChartsBuilder`, `InsightsCalculator`, `ScreenTimeProvider`, or any chart geometry, the recurring defect class here is TWO sources of truth for one number.**

## Feature summary

- **"Walked Away" tracking** — counts when user taps "I changed my mind" instead of waiting
- **2x2 dashboard stats** — Screen Time, Active Rules (tappable), Blocked, Walked Away

## Stats visualization architecture

- `ui/screens/stats/StatsCalculator.kt` — pure Kotlin (no Android deps), injected via Hilt. Methods: `buildWeeklyDataFromTotals`, `buildTrendData`, `buildAppTrendData`, `calculateStreak`. Fully unit-testable. The event-summing `buildWeeklyData`/`buildHourlyData` pair was deleted together with `durationMs` (#22) — screen-time series come from `ScreenTimeProvider` (UsageStatsManager) and reach the charts as pre-computed totals; `usage_events` only feeds the blocked/walked-away counts and the streak. Every day boundary it walks is `TimeTracker.startOfDayDaysBefore`, never `i * 86_400_000` — see "One events-based source" below.
- `ui/screens/stats/charts/WeeklyBarChart.kt` — Canvas-based 7-day bar chart with rounded corners, day labels
- `ui/screens/stats/charts/BlockedTrendChart.kt` — dual chart: bars (blocked) + line with dots (walked away)
- `ui/screens/stats/charts/HourlyHeatmap.kt` — 24-cell row, color intensity from surfaceVariant to primary
- `ui/screens/stats/charts/StreakCounter.kt` — flame icon + streak count + "X days streak" label
- `ui/screens/stats/charts/ChartGeometry.kt` — the ONE bar-layout/hit-test arithmetic (`barWidth`/`barLeft`/`barIndexAt`), pure + unit-tested. It used to be copy-pasted into each chart's `detectTapGestures` **and** separately into its `Canvas` draw block, so a chart could hit-test against a layout it was not drawing. Label rows must use `Arrangement.spacedBy(BAR_SPACING)` + `weight(1f)` cells for the same reason — `SpaceBetween` gives cells of `width/count` against bars of `(width − totalSpacing)/count`, and the two drift apart across the row.
- All charts use Material 3 colorScheme exclusively, handle empty states, no external dependencies.

### One events-based source for every day-scoped screen-time number

Reported from device QA as *"a Wednesday bar renders tall and dark, and drilling into that Wednesday shows a hero total of 0s, 'No usage recorded' and an empty app list."* Both halves were internally consistent and flatly contradicted each other, because they were two different computations of one calendar day: `getDailyScreenTimesForWeek` built the bars from `queryUsageStats(INTERVAL_DAILY)` — the pre-aggregated buckets `ScreenTimeProvider`'s own comment already called stale and midnight-misaligned on Android 12+ — while the drill-down summed live `queryEvents` ACTIVITY_RESUMED→PAUSED spans. The sibling of the day-selection bug below: two sources of truth for one question, and the louder one was wrong.

- **`domain/usage/DailyUsageAccumulator.kt`** (pure) is the one place spans become days. It pairs RESUMED with PAUSED and splits each span across the day buckets it overlaps, against **supplied** boundaries. A second RESUMED without a PAUSED replaces the first; a PAUSED with no open RESUMED is ignored; a backwards pair (the platform does emit them) is worth **zero, never a negative bar**. A span still open at the end of the stream counts **up to now**, and only when the window actually reaches the present — for a window that already ended, the closing PAUSED merely fell outside the query, and inventing an end would credit a past day with time we cannot see.
- **`ScreenTimeProvider.getWeeklyUsage(lastDayStartMs)`** is the only screen-time series in the app. **ONE `queryEvents` pass** over the whole window feeds the accumulator. Seven per-day queries would cost seven binder round-trips on every 30 s Home/Stats poll on a 3 GB Pixel 3 **and** could not see a session crossing midnight at all — each half would be an unpaired event in its own day's query and would be dropped from both days.
- **Day boundaries are `TimeTracker.startOfDayDaysBefore`**, calendar arithmetic, so they are true local midnights and a DST day is genuinely 23 or 25 hours wide. `startOfDayDaysBefore(dayStart, -1)` walks *forward* one day — that is how the window's exclusive end is derived. `StatsCalculator` moved to the same helper: its labels and its blocked/walked-away buckets are drawn beside these bars and must cover the same hours, or on two days a year one chart's "Wed" would be a different 24 hours from its neighbour's.
- **The screens do not recompute a day, they read a column.** `WeeklyUsage` carries per-day *per-app* totals: the bars are `dailyTotals()`, the drill-down's hero total and app list are `perAppOn(dayStart)`, App Detail's bars are `dailyTotalsFor(pkg)`, and Home's "Screen Time" tile is `totalOn(todayStart)` — today's cell of the very series its own mini chart draws. Agreement is structural, not a discipline two implementations are expected to keep. The day-scoped provider APIs that made a second computation possible (`getTotalScreenTime`, `getPerAppScreenTime`, and the `…Today` wrappers) were **deleted**; `getPerAppSessionStats` stays because a *range* with session counts (Willpower's mean session length) is a genuinely different question.
- **A day is addressed by its start timestamp, never by an index into whatever is loaded.** `_selection` moves the instant an arrow is tapped while the new window is still in flight, so for that frame an index would resolve to a different date — one day's numbers under another day's heading, the defect all over again. `perAppOn` returns **empty** for a day the loaded window does not hold, and the bars are labelled from `weeklyUsage.lastDayStartMs` (the window the data actually covers) rather than from the selection.
- The selected day's **hourly heatmap** is the one day-scoped read that remains: a within-day breakdown is a different question, and carrying 7×24 buckets for the six days nobody is looking at is not worth it.
- **Tests**: `DailyUsageAccumulatorTest` (21 — one-day spans, one and two midnight crossings, open-span-to-now vs dropped, clamping at both window edges, malformed sequences, per-app isolation, both DST days, and the partition property that makes reading a column safe), `WeeklyUsageTest` (11 — the accessors, a day outside the window reading empty rather than a neighbour, and `WEEK_DAYS == StatsDaySelection.WINDOW_DAYS`), `ScreenTimeSourceContractTest` (5 — source-level, since the ViewModels are not JVM-testable: no `INTERVAL_DAILY`, no day-scoped total API beside the weekly one, exactly one weekly read per screen, timestamp addressing, and no raw 24-hour day arithmetic left in any file that buckets a day; it strips comments before scanning so documenting the defect cannot fail the test that prevents it). Mutation-checked: replacing the split with "credit the day it started in" fails 7 of them.

### One app is in the foreground at a time — the 17-hour day (v1.15.1)

Reported from Anti's daily-driver phone the day after v1.15.0 shipped: *"~17 hours of screen time today, across a few apps"*, before lunchtime. The v1.15.0 change above moved every screen-time number off `INTERVAL_DAILY` and onto our own event pairing — which was the right move, and it shipped with an accounting hole the pre-aggregated buckets had never had.

Three read paths (`getWeeklyUsage`, `getHourlyScreenTime`/`getPerAppHourlyScreenTime`, `getPerAppSessionStats`) each walked the cursor with their **own** `package -> startTime` map. A map holds one open span *per package*, so nothing in the model said only one app can be on screen; and every span still open at the end was credited from its RESUMED **to now**, unbounded. Measured against the v1.15.0 code:

| Scenario | v1.15.0 said | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Two apps, one missing `ACTIVITY_PAUSED` | **16 h** on that day | 2 h |
| Four unclosed apps, 11 h into the day | **38 h** today | ≤ 11 h |
| One lost close event 3 days ago | **75 h** across the week, days 4/5/6 at 24/24/12 | ≤ 4 h |
| Phone watched to 22:00, screen off overnight | **9 h** already today at 09:00 | 0 h |

- **`domain/usage/ForegroundSpanTracker.kt`** (pure) is now the one answer to "which app is foreground, from when", and it emits **non-overlapping** spans. A RESUMED for a *different* package closes the open one at that instant — the app you left stopped being foreground exactly when the next one started. That is what makes "a day cannot exceed 24 hours, today cannot exceed the time since midnight" **structural** rather than a clamp bolted on afterwards; there is no clamp on the totals, and there deliberately isn't one, because a clamped total over unclamped per-app entries would put the bar and its drill-down back into disagreement.
- **Everything that ends the foreground ends the span**: `ACTIVITY_PAUSED`, the `ACTIVITY_STOPPED` of the *same activity class* that opened it (matched on class, because an in-app transition emits `A PAUSED, B RESUMED, A STOPPED` and reading that trailing event by package alone would close B's fresh span), `SCREEN_NON_INTERACTIVE`, `KEYGUARD_SHOWN`, `DEVICE_SHUTDOWN`. The screen-off pair is the one that matters most in the field: a phone put down mid-session frequently never delivers the app's own PAUSED.
- **An open span is INFERRED, so it is capped** (`DEFAULT_OPEN_TAIL_LIMIT_MS`, 4 h). Only its start was observed. A span whose end we actually saw is never shortened, however long — the cap applies to inference, not to measurement.
- **`ScreenTimeProvider` no longer interprets events.** One private `feed(events, tracker)` is the only `while (events.hasNextEvent())` loop in the file; `DailyUsageAccumulator` (day buckets), `HourlyUsageAccumulator` (heatmap cells, moved out of the provider's old private `distributeToHours` so it is JVM-testable) and the session-stats map are all just consumers of the same spans. `getPerAppHourlyScreenTime` used to `continue` past other packages' events *before* pairing, which blinded it to exactly the signal that closes a span; it now filters the resulting spans, not the stream.
- **Nothing is persisted**, so the fix is retroactive: every screen-time number is recomputed from `UsageStatsManager` on each read (`usage_events` holds block decisions only, never durations — issue #22). No migration, no stored aggregate to repair.
- **Tests**: `ForegroundSpanTrackerTest` (18 — app-switch-without-PAUSED, the three-apps-one-morning case, the non-overlap property over a deliberately malformed stream, screen-off, the cap vs. a measured long session, STOPPED class matching in both directions, malformed pairs), `HourlyUsageAccumulatorTest` (9), `DailyUsageAccumulatorTest` (+6, incl. the two day-length invariants and the stale-RESUMED case; the old `packages are kept apart` case **asserted the bug** — two apps RESUMED at 09:00 producing 3 h out of 2 wall-clock hours — and was rewritten), `ScreenTimeSourceContractTest` (+1: no `foregroundStarts` map in the provider, exactly one event loop).

### Day selection is CONTROLLED — the chart and the numbers are one state

Reported as *"I click a different day in weekly trends, it shows as selected, but it doesn't swap the numbers to that day."* `WeeklyBarChart` and `BlockedTrendChart` each owned a private `var selectedIndex by remember { mutableStateOf<Int?>(null) }`; tapping a bar dimmed its neighbours and printed a tooltip and did **nothing else**, while the screen's real day lived in the ViewModel and moved only via the date arrows. Two sources of truth for one question, and the louder one was inert.

- **`ui/screens/stats/StatsDaySelection.kt`** — pure `java.time` (no Android, no Compose state), the single answer to "which day am I looking at". Two fields, one invariant: `selected` always lies inside the 7-day window ending at `weekEnd`, and `selectedIndex` is where it falls among the bars.
  - **Tapping a bar moves `selected` only** — the window stays put, so bars never slide out from under the finger. **Arrows** move `selected` and slide the window by the *minimum* needed to keep it visible. `nextDay` is capped at today; `selectIndex` clamps both the index and a future day; `jumpToToday` resets both fields.
  - The streak is anchored on `weekEnd`, **not** `selected`: a streak is a "how am I doing right now" number and scrubbing back to inspect a Tuesday must not rewrite it.
  - `StatsDateLabels` (same file) owns all date wording — `day()` ("Today"/"Yesterday"/"Thu, Aug 21") and `range()` ("Last 7 days", or explicit dates once scrolled back) — so a day is never called "Today" in one place and printed as a date in another. `StatsViewModel.formatDateLabel` is retained as a thin delegate to `StatsDateLabels.full`.
- **The charts take `selectedIndex` + `onSelectDay` and own no selection state.** `onSelectDay = null` means "read-only": **no `pointerInput` is installed at all**, so taps fall through to a parent card. That is exactly how the home dashboard embeds them.
- **`DayNavigationHeader`** (in `StatsScreen.kt`, shared with `AppDetailScreen`) carries the day in words + which window the charts below are drawing + a "Back to today" chip that renders **only** when it can do something (its presence IS the "you are not on today" signal). The selected bar draws solid with a bold label and a marker dot.
- The selected day's Blocked / Walked-away counts sit directly under the trend chart — that pair of numbers is literally what the report was about, so they are rendered next to the chart rather than a scroll away.
- **ViewModel flow shape** (`StatsViewModel`, `AppDetailViewModel`): the events query and the weekly screen-time series key on `weekEnd` (`map { }.distinctUntilChanged().flatMapLatest { }`), the day series keys on `selected`. Tapping between bars therefore re-slices a list already in memory instead of re-subscribing to Room.
- **`StatsViewModel.Companion.polled(isLive) { … }`** is the shared UsageStatsManager poller (`AppDetailViewModel` imports it). It runs on **`Dispatchers.IO`** — `getDailyScreenTimesForWeek` is seven binder round-trips and this feeds `stateIn(viewModelScope)`, i.e. the main thread — and a window that has **already ended emits once and completes** rather than re-reading seven identical queries every 30 s forever.
- Tests: `StatsDaySelectionTest` (18 — every transition, window sliding both directions, the today cap, index round-trip, out-of-range clamping, the "selected is always inside the window it draws" invariant over interleaved taps and arrows, label rules), `ChartGeometryTest` (8, incl. the 24-bar dense case).

### Home dashboard charts

`ui/screens/home/HomeChartsBuilder.kt` (pure, Hilt-injected, `HomeChartsBuilderTest` 9 cases) turns the weekly screen-time totals + the week's `usage_events` into the two mini charts on `HomeScreen`'s "Last 7 days" card (screen-time bars; blocks-vs-walk-aways trend). It delegates to `StatsCalculator`, so home and the stats screen can never bucket a day differently. Both charts are read-only (`onSelectDay = null`) and the whole card opens Usage Stats — the home screen has nowhere to display a day selection.

- Cost: one extra windowed Room flow + one extra UsageStats read folded into the **existing** 30 s poll, and the poll moved to `Dispatchers.IO` (it was doing binder reads on the main thread).
- **`HomeViewModel.dayStartFlow`**: `todayStart` used to be a `val` computed in the constructor, so a phone left on the home screen over midnight kept counting yesterday's events under a heading that said "Today" — the same "label and numbers disagree" defect as the chart bug. It is now re-derived on the poll tick with `distinctUntilChanged`, so the downstream Room queries re-subscribe once a day, not every 30 s.
- The "Screen Time" tile was inert once usage permission was granted (the one tile showing a number the stats screen exists to explain). It now opens Usage Stats.

### Insight pages (v1.12.0) — Willpower + Interventions, reached from the home tiles

The home dashboard's "Blocked" / "Walked Away" tiles (both rows) navigate to `WillpowerScreen` / `InterventionsScreen` (`ui/screens/stats/`, routes in `NavGraph`). All math lives in **`InsightsCalculator.kt`** — pure Kotlin, `ZoneId` passed in, bucketing via java.time (37 tests in `InsightsCalculatorTest`).

- **The denominator invariant.** A walk-away logs a SECOND `usage_events` row (`wasBlocked=1, userChangedMind=1`) alongside the show-time row, so raw `wasBlocked=1` counts ≈ double-count walk-aways. The calculator defines `attempts = max(shown, walkAways)`, `gaveIn = attempts − walkAways`, `rate = walkAways/attempts` — >100%/NaN/negative are structurally impossible, and `walkAways + gaveIn == attempts` holds per bucket. All-time hero numbers route through `overlaysFromAllTimeCounts(blocked, changedMind)` (regression-tested); the home tiles' raw counts are deliberately untouched.
- **Time reclaimed** = walkAways × per-app mean session length from `ScreenTimeProvider.getPerAppSessionStats` (the primitive `getPerAppScreenTime` now wraps; still one `queryEvents` pass), clamped 1–30 min, 5-min labeled default when unmeasured. Always "est.".
- Charts (`charts/`): `WalkAwayRing`, `RateBarChart` (24-bar rate-by-hour, volume-weighted alpha; bar and label rows must share the same spacing math), `SegmentedBar`, `Sparkline`, `WeekHourHeatmap` (7×24). `InstalledAppsRepository.resolveIcon` caches icons incl. negative misses for uninstalled apps.
- Weekly trend: 30d = 4 buckets, oldest absorbs the 2 remainder days; 7d range shows a hint instead of a one-point trend. Hours with <2 attempts are excluded from strongest/weakest-hour callouts.
- **Known gap**: `UsageRepository.cleanup(retainDays=30)` has NO call site — documented retention is not enforced, events accumulate indefinitely. The insight pages are correct either way (they window in the calculator), but "30-day retention" is currently aspirational.
